Quantum computing represents a paradigm shift in computational theory, leveraging the principles of superposition and entanglement to perform calculations that exceed the capabilities of classical von Neumann architectures.

In this paper, we propose a novel approach to error correction in superconducting qubits. By implementing a dynamic decoupling sequence, we demonstrate a 40% increase in coherence time compared to standard baseline measurements.

The implications for cryptography are profound, as Shor's algorithm could potentially render current RSA encryption obsolete within the next decade if scalable hardware targets are met. This necessitates the development of post-quantum cryptographic standards.

Our experimental results were obtained using a 54-qubit processor operating at millikelvin temperatures. We observed significant improvements in gate fidelity after implementing our custom calibration protocol. The mean error rate decreased from 2.1% to 0.8%.

Furthermore, we investigated the scalability of our approach by simulating systems of up to 1000 qubits using classical tensor network methods. These simulations suggest that our error correction scheme maintains its effectiveness as the system size increases, making it a promising candidate for fault-tolerant quantum computation.
